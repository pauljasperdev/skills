# Writing the hook

The first three seconds decide whether anyone sees the rest, and most viewers watch without sound. So the hook is three things that must say the same thing, in this order of weight: the text on screen, what is shown, and the spoken first sentence. A viewer who reads one message and hears another hesitates, and a hesitating viewer swipes.

A topic label is not a hook. "Game Changer / Feste Kamera" names the subject; "Handy aufbauen nervt?" tells one kind of viewer that this is about their problem. The user rejected six label hooks on 2026-10-05 and supplied the rules below.

## What a hook has to do

- **Relevance:** the viewer knows at once whether this is for them. Name the viewer or their problem.
- **Payoff:** what they get, concretely. A real number or a visible result beats an adjective.
- **Tension:** a reason to stay. Say what the thing is; hold back how or why. Curiosity comes from contrast between what the viewer believes and what the short claims.
- **Proof:** something that makes the claim believable, shown rather than asserted.

Make the value more immediate, never the wording more dramatic. Every claim stays as true as the recording: a question the speaker raises stays a question on screen.

## Text hook

Three to seven words, one idea, readable without sound. It states the problem or result with a specific qualifier and does not merely repeat the spoken sentence. In `edit.json` it is the `hook` list: two lines of at most 19 characters, both in the one hook style of the house style.

Angles that work, one per short: the problem ("Du verlierst X wegen Y"), the mistake ("Hör auf mit X"), the payoff ("X ohne Y"), the contrarian claim, a real test result, an open question whose answer is not obvious, a demonstration.

## Visual hook

Show the evidence while the hook is spoken: the result, the before and after, the screenshot, the product, the book. In this workspace that means an overlay from the first frame, like the tweet in `058`. Prefer, in this order: the result, evidence, a demonstration, a relevant picture, and only then decoration. Movement alone is not a visual hook.

## Spoken hook

The recording fixes it, but the cut chooses where it starts. Remove the lead-in ("So,", "Also,") and open on the interesting sentence. When a stronger sentence sits later in the take, propose opening with it: moving a sentence is a substantive change the user approves, like any deletion. When the opening cannot carry the hook, say so; the user can record one.

## Check before building

- Is the topic obvious in the first second, with the sound off?
- Is a specific viewer or problem named?
- Is there a concrete payoff and a reason to keep watching?
- Do text, picture and first sentence mean the same thing?
- Does the short deliver the promise quickly, and is every claim accurate?
