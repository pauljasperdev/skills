# Sources and numbers

## Claims

- Check every claim against a suitable source, above all numbers, motives, causal explanations and company histories. An anecdote is checked like a valuation argument.
- The link sits at the statement. What changes over time carries its date.
- Paul's thoughts open the search; they are neither evidence nor its limit.
- When the source does not carry the statement, narrow or correct the statement.
- „Anders als oft gedacht" needs evidence that it is often thought.
- An expectation about the future belongs to whoever holds it: the company's guidance, a named analyst consensus, a named expert, each with its date.
- An accusation against a company or a person (fraud, misconduct, a lawsuit) stays with the source that makes it, with its status: alleged, charged, ruled.

## Expert and podcast opinions

Keep speaker, show, episode, date, timestamp and main reasoning together, and check the context in the original; a search hit or an automatic transcript does not settle what was meant. An opinion stays with its speaker and its date. Your own inference is never attributed to an expert.

## Valuation and price

- Every market figure has its company, source and Stichtag.
- A revenue multiple says whether it uses market value or enterprise value, and trailing or expected revenue. A comparison uses one basis and one period for all companies.
- Rounded magnitudes are fine in speech, with the comparison named: lower than before, or than which competitors.
- „Ein wichtiger Grund" needs evidence; a plausible inference stays „könnte erklären".
- A lower valuation is not undervaluation, a multiple alone is no fair price, a risen share price is no valuation measure, and one company's valuation is not its industry's.
- Missing data stays a named gap.
